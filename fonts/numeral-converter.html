Why Devanagari Numerals Look Different from the Numbers You Use Every Day

The "0-9" digits used across most of the world today — including in everyday Marathi text, prices, and phone numbers — are technically called Arabic (or Hindu-Arabic) numerals, and they're used almost universally regardless of script or language. Devanagari, the script Marathi and Hindi are written in, has its own historical numeral system: ० १ २ ३ ४ ५ ६ ७ ८ ९. These aren't a font variation of 0-9 — they're a separate set of Unicode characters with their own code points, which is why this tool exists as a proper character converter rather than something you can achieve just by changing the font.

In modern Marathi writing, Devanagari numerals have become mostly ceremonial. Everyday digital communication — WhatsApp messages, invoices, website prices, even most Marathi newspapers — uses standard 0-9 because it's what every keyboard, calculator, and payment system defaults to. But Devanagari numerals still appear regularly in specific, deliberate contexts: wedding invitations, traditional calendars (पंचांग), religious texts, government seals, and stylised branding where the goal is visual and cultural authenticity rather than everyday convenience.

Where to Use Devanagari Numerals

  • Wedding cards and invitations. Dates and addresses set in Devanagari numerals alongside Devanagari text read as more visually cohesive and traditional than mixing scripts.
  • Festival and religious content. Panchang dates, temple signage, and religious publications commonly use Devanagari numerals as a matter of convention.
  • Decorative and branding use. A shop sign, a logo, or a poster designed entirely in Devanagari script often looks more consistent with matching Devanagari numerals rather than switching to 0-9 mid-design.
  • Government and formal documents. Some official Marathi-language documents and stamps use Devanagari numerals, particularly in Maharashtra state government contexts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Marathi numerals different from Hindi or Sanskrit numerals?

No — Marathi, Hindi, and Sanskrit all use the same set of Devanagari numerals (०-९), since they share the Devanagari script. This converter works identically for all three.

Where are Devanagari numerals actually used today?

Mainly in formal or traditional Marathi print — invitations, calendars, official documents, religious texts, and stylised designs — while everyday digital use (phone numbers, prices, dates in apps) almost always defaults to standard Arabic numerals even within Marathi text.

Can I convert a mix of numbers and regular text at once?

Yes — paste a full sentence or paragraph containing numbers anywhere in it, and only the digits will be converted; all other text passes through unchanged.
