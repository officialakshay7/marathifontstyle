How This Marathi to English Converter Works

This tool does the reverse of the more commonly used direction: instead of typing English and getting Devanagari, you paste Devanagari Marathi text and get back a readable, phonetic English-letter spelling. It reads each consonant, vowel sign (matra), and modifier in your Marathi text and maps it to the closest Roman letters — so "नमस्कार" becomes "namaskaar," letter by letter, sound by sound.

This is transliteration, not translation. The output tells you how a Marathi word sounds when read aloud in the Roman alphabet — it does not tell you what the word means in English. It's built for situations where someone needs to read or pronounce Marathi text but doesn't read Devanagari script fluently, or where a name, address, or phrase needs to be represented in Roman letters for a form, a caption, or an international audience.

Common Uses for Marathi Romanization

  • Names and addresses on international forms. Passport applications, visa forms, and international shipping often require Marathi names to appear in Roman letters.
  • Captions and subtitles. Adding a readable Roman-letter version of spoken or written Marathi alongside the original script, for viewers who don't read Devanagari.
  • Teaching and learning. Helping someone learning Marathi sound out words before they're comfortable reading the script fluently.
  • Search and typing convenience. Making Marathi words findable or typeable in English-first systems and keyboards.

A Note on Accuracy

Devanagari-to-Roman conversion doesn't have one single universal standard — academic transliteration systems like IAST use special diacritical marks (ā, ī, ś) for precision, while everyday informal romanization (the style most people actually type on WhatsApp) uses only plain English letters, sometimes doubling vowels for length ("aa," "ee," "oo"). This tool favors the second, more readable style. For formal or academic use where exact diacritics matter, treat this as a solid starting draft rather than a final citation-ready transliteration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this the same as translating Marathi to English?

No — this is transliteration, not translation. It converts the sounds of Marathi words into the closest Roman-letter spelling, so a reader who doesn't know Devanagari script can still read the word phonetically. It does not tell you what the word means in English.

Why does the output sometimes look different from how I'd spell it myself?

Devanagari-to-Roman conversion is a best-effort phonetic match, not a single fixed standard — there are several competing romanization conventions, so the same Marathi word can validly romanize a few different ways. This tool uses simple, readable spellings rather than academic transliteration marks.

Can I convert English/Roman text back into Marathi Unicode?

Yes — use our Unicode Converter for the opposite direction: type in English transliteration and get proper Devanagari Marathi text.

Is my text stored or sent anywhere?

No — the conversion happens entirely in your browser using JavaScript running on your own device. Nothing you type is uploaded, stored, or logged anywhere.
