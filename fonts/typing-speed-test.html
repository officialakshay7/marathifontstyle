How This Marathi Typing Test Works

A prompt sentence in Devanagari Marathi appears above the input box. The timer starts the moment you type your first character, and you have 60 seconds to type as much of the prompt — and any prompts that follow — as accurately as you can. Correctly typed characters turn the counter forward; your words-per-minute (WPM) and accuracy percentage update live as you go, and a final result appears once the time runs out.

This is a practice tool, not a certified typing exam — it's meant to help you build comfort and speed with a Devanagari keyboard layout over repeated attempts, the same way any typing tutor works for English.

Tips to Type Marathi Faster

  • Use a phonetic keyboard layout if you're a beginner. Phonetic (transliteration-based) Devanagari keyboards map roughly to how a word sounds in English letters, which is far easier to learn quickly than the traditional Inscript layout.
  • Don't look at the keyboard. Like any typing skill, speed comes from muscle memory — practising without looking at your keys, even slowly at first, builds speed faster than typing fast while looking down.
  • Practice matras and conjuncts specifically. Devanagari vowel signs (matras) and conjunct consonants are usually where typing speed drops — deliberately practising words with these is more useful than practising simple words you already type quickly.
  • Short, frequent sessions beat long, rare ones. Ten focused minutes a day for two weeks will improve your typing speed more reliably than one long session.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's a good Marathi typing speed?

For most everyday use, 20-30 WPM in Devanagari is comfortable and practical. Professional typists and data-entry roles often reach 35-50+ WPM with practice. Devanagari typing tends to run slower than English typing for the same person, since Devanagari requires more keystrokes per character for matras and conjuncts.

Do I need a Marathi keyboard installed to take this test?

You need some way to type Devanagari characters — either an operating-system Marathi/Devanagari keyboard layout (Inscript or phonetic), a phone's Gboard/SwiftKey with Marathi enabled, or you can type the prompt using our Unicode Converter first and paste the result in as practice.

Is my typing data stored or sent anywhere?

No — the test runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is uploaded, stored, or logged anywhere; your WPM and accuracy are calculated locally and disappear when you leave the page.
