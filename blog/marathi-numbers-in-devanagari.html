Marathi Numbers in Devanagari — Complete Guide

Everything you need to know about ०-९, the Devanagari numeral system used in Marathi — where they come from, where they're still used, and how to convert them.

7 min read·2026-08-19

If you've spent any time around Marathi print — a wedding card, a religious calendar, an old government stamp — you've probably noticed numbers that don't look like the 0-9 you type every day. Those are Devanagari numerals: ० १ २ ३ ४ ५ ६ ७ ८ ९. They belong to the same script family as Marathi and Hindi text itself, and while they've mostly stepped aside for standard Arabic numerals in everyday digital life, they haven't disappeared — they've just moved into more specific, deliberate roles.

This guide covers where Devanagari numerals came from, exactly how each digit maps to 0-9, where you'll still genuinely encounter them today, and how to convert between the two systems without needing to switch your keyboard layout.

The Full Devanagari Numeral Set

0 = ०  ·  1 = १  ·  2 = २  ·  3 = ३  ·  4 = ४
5 = ५  ·  6 = ६  ·  7 = ७  ·  8 = ८  ·  9 = ९

Like the Arabic numeral system, Devanagari numerals are positional — a multi-digit number is built the same way you'd build any number, just with different digit shapes. So 2026 becomes २०२६, 42 becomes ४२, and so on. There's no separate word-based counting system involved; it's a direct digit-for-digit swap.

Where Devanagari Numerals Are Still Used

  • Wedding invitations and formal cards. A card set entirely in Devanagari script often uses matching Devanagari numerals for dates and addresses, for visual consistency.
  • Traditional calendars (पंचांग). Marathi almanacs and religious calendars commonly display dates in Devanagari numerals as a matter of convention.
  • Religious and classical texts. Verse numbering and page references in traditional or religious publications frequently use Devanagari numerals.
  • Government and heritage signage. Some Maharashtra state government documents, seals, and heritage-site signage use Devanagari numerals, particularly in formal or ceremonial contexts.
  • Decorative and brand design. Logos, posters, and shop signage designed fully in Devanagari script sometimes carry the numerals through for a cohesive look.

Outside these contexts — phone numbers, prices, dates in apps, casual messaging — Marathi speakers use standard 0-9 by default, the same as almost every other language written digitally today. That's not a sign Devanagari numerals are disappearing; it's simply that digital keyboards, calculators, and payment systems were built around Arabic numerals globally, and switching input methods for numbers specifically isn't practical for everyday use.

A Quick History

Devanagari numerals share a common ancestor with Arabic numerals — both trace back to the Hindu-Arabic numeral system that originated in India roughly 1,500 years ago and spread westward through the Islamic world into Europe, where the digit shapes evolved into the 0-9 forms used globally today. In India, the numeral shapes themselves evolved differently across regions and scripts, which is why Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, and other Indian scripts each ended up with their own distinct digit forms, even though they all share the same underlying positional, base-10 structure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Marathi numerals the same as Hindi numerals?

Yes — Marathi and Hindi both use the Devanagari script and the same set of numerals (०-९), since the numeral system belongs to the script, not the individual language.

Do Marathi speakers actually use ०-९ in daily life?

Rarely in casual digital use — phones, WhatsApp, and most websites default to standard 0-9. Devanagari numerals mostly appear in formal, traditional, or decorative contexts today, such as invitations, calendars, and religious texts.
