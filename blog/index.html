Marathi Font Guides

Expert articles on Marathi and Devanagari typography — choosing fonts, installing them, and using them well.

Welcome to our collection of Marathi and Devanagari typography guides. Whether you're picking a font for a wedding card, trying to fix broken Kruti Dev text, designing a YouTube thumbnail, or just curious where the Devanagari script came from, these articles are written to be genuinely useful rather than padded — practical steps, honest comparisons, and free tools you can use right away. Every guide below is free to read, updated for 2026, and written by people who actually use these fonts and tools day to day.

New to Marathi typography? Start with our complete guide to Devanagari font categories or the history of the Devanagari script for background. Already know what you're looking for? Jump straight to a use-case guide — wedding invitations, Instagram bios, YouTube thumbnails and channel art, PowerPoint slides, logos, or converting old Kruti Dev and DevLys documents to modern Unicode.

These guides are grouped loosely into a few categories: font selection guides that help you pick the right typeface for a specific project, technical guides that explain how Unicode, Kruti Dev, Shree Lipi, and DevLys actually differ under the hood, platform tutorials for using Marathi fonts correctly in Word, PowerPoint, Instagram, and YouTube, and a couple of deeper reads on the calligraphy craft and the script's history for anyone curious about where all of this typography actually comes from. We add new articles regularly as new platforms, font releases, and common questions come up, so it's worth checking back or bookmarking this page if Marathi typography is something you work with often.

Every guide on this page links back to our free tools where relevant — the font generator, the Unicode converter, and the Kruti Dev converter — so you can move from reading about a technique straight into using it without hunting for the right page separately.

If you can't find a guide for your specific situation — a platform we haven't covered yet, an unusual legacy font, or a design use case that doesn't fit neatly into an existing article — reach out through our Contact page. Reader questions are one of the main ways we decide what to write about next.
